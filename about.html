About Benchnote

Lab time is short and a trainer board is not always in reach. Benchnote puts a breadboard and a full set of bench instruments in your browser, so you can build and test any circuit whenever you want: before a lab, for a project idea, or just to see what happens.

Why Benchnote exists

Benchnote is a free virtual electronics lab for electrical and electronic engineering students. Its main purpose is simple: give every student a breadboard they can practise on anytime, without needing a trainer kit, a lab slot or even a computer. You can build any digital or analog circuit, test it with real bench instruments and see exactly where it goes wrong. The most common lab experiments come ready-made as examples, with tools to find faults, practise for lab exams and check your work, so that you walk into the real lab already confident.

The practice breadboard

This is the heart of Benchnote. The practice breadboard has every part in one place: logic chips next to resistors, transistors, op-amps, a 555 timer and a 7805 regulator, with a power supply, function generator, multimeter, ammeters and an oscilloscope. The Digital and Analog labs each have their own free breadboard with just their own parts.

  • Digital: 30 TTL chips from gates to counters and shift registers, with 8 switches, 8 LEDs, a 7-segment display, a clock, pulsers and a logic probe. The trainer tells you about unpowered chips, floating inputs, shorts and outputs fighting each other.
  • Analog: resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, LEDs, Zeners, transistors and the 741 op-amp, with a ±12 V supply, two variable supplies, a function generator, a multimeter, two ammeters, a two-channel oscilloscope, switches, a current source and a potentiometer.
  • Tap a part onto a hole, drag wires between any two points, and your board is saved on your device.

Ready-made examples

The most common lab experiments are already built for you as examples: logic gates, adders, decoders, flip-flops and counters on the digital side; Ohm's law, network theorems, a Wheatstone bridge, filters, rectifiers, transistors, op-amps, a 555 blinker and a 7805 supply on the analog side. Each one shows the aim and theory, the circuit wired step by step, and an empty board where you can build it yourself and have it checked. They are a starting point, not a limit: anything you can wire, you can test.

Practise like it's the real lab

  • Find the fault: every example can be loaded with one hidden mistake, such as a wire one hole off, a missing ground, a wrong resistor or a diode in backwards. Track it down with the meters, fix it and check.
  • Lab exam practice: a random experiment, an empty board and a timer, graded when you submit.
  • Observation table and graph: record readings as you vary a source and plot them straight away.

Also included

  • K-Map solver for two to six variables, with prime implicants and the tabular working.
  • Tools for colour codes, dividers, filters, op-amp gain, the 555 timer and number bases.
  • Breadboard basics with a pinout for every chip, and a printable revision sheet.

How the simulation works

The digital trainer reduces the breadboard to electrical nodes and evaluates every chip until the circuit settles. The analog trainer solves the circuit with modified nodal analysis and Newton–Raphson iteration, the same family of methods SPICE uses, with simplified device models.

Results are close to a real bench but not identical. Real components have tolerances and real wires come loose. Use Benchnote to understand and rehearse, and trust your lab instruments on the day.
